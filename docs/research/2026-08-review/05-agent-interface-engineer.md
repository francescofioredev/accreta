## F-AIE-01: check_drift is quadratic (pages x changed-paths), not linear, and no benchmark covers it - it is the largest unbounded response on the surface
- Severity: high
- Evidence: MEASURED. packages/core/src/source/drift.ts duplicates the ENTIRE changedPaths array into EVERY stale page: `for (const path of pages) { stale.push({ path, verifiedAt: revision, changedPaths }); }`. P pages sharing one stale revision against a source reporting C changed paths yields P x C path strings in one response. Measurement harness in my own scratch directory, serialising exactly as server.ts:29 does, using a STUB ADAPTER - legitimate, because detectDrift only ever calls revision() and changedSince(). Same token method and bias as bench/mcp-budget.ts. darwin arm64, bun 1.3.13:
    pages | changed | bytes | est tokens | % of 200k
    10    | 10      | 5.8KB  | 1.5k   | 0.7%
    100   | 10      | 56.2KB | 14.4k  | 7.2%
    100   | 100     | 416.5KB| 106.6k | 53.3%
    1000  | 10      | 559.8KB| 143.3k | 71.7%
    1000  | 100     | 4.1MB  | 1065.8k| 533%
    1000  | 1000    | 39.3MB | 10290.8k| 5145%
  Holding pages at 1000 and moving C 10->100->1000 multiplies bytes 559.8KB->4.1MB->39.3MB. That is the product term, MEASURED, not extrapolated.
- Where: packages/core/src/source/drift.ts (the for-loop); surfaced unbounded by packages/mcp-server/src/tools.ts:62-81, registered at server.ts:107-117
- Failure scenario: A git-sourced KB of 1,000 pages, all verified at the same commit. Someone merges a branch touching 100 files. The agent calls check_drift with no arguments - THE DOCUMENTED WAY TO CHECK EVERYTHING, since source is optional. The server returns a 4.1MB single text block, ~1.07M estimated tokens, FIVE TIMES a 200k window. The agent does not get a truncated answer it can work around; it gets a context-window overflow or a hard transport failure. The user sees the drift check fail at exactly the moment drift detection is the thing they needed. Note against bench/mcp-budget.ts's model: lint and find_consumers are linear in findings, so 1,000 pages is 20% of a window; check_drift at the same 1,000 pages with a 100-file commit is 533%. IT CROSSES THE CLIFF ROUGHLY 25x EARLIER than the tools the benchmark covers.
- Falsifiable proposal: (1) NORMALISE THE REPORT - emit changedPaths once per stale REVISION rather than once per page: stale: [{revision, changedPaths, pages:[...]}]. The data ALREADY GROUPS THAT WAY (byRevision is built in drift.ts and then flattened). Removes the P multiplier entirely; a pure serialisation change invalidating nothing about the three-way outcome. (2) Bound and paginate independently of (1). WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: if on real KBs the number of DISTINCT stale revisions is close to the number of stale pages (each page verified at its own revision), grouping buys nothing and the P multiplier was never real - measure the distribution of last_verified_revision cardinality over pages. My harness deliberately used the worst case; I did NOT measure the real distribution.
- Cost to verify: 1-2h. The grouping change is contained to one struct plus its consumers. The falsification test needs a real multi-page KB with populated knowledge/, which accreta-atlas deliberately does not have (verified empty).
- Confidence: high for the measurement and the quadratic mechanism. MEDIUM for the real-world frequency, because the shared-revision worst case is an assumption about usage I did not measure - though it is what a git source produces naturally, since every page ingested in one run records the same HEAD.

## F-AIE-02: The reproduced budget numbers hold, but the headline get_page figure is a benchmark artifact and should not be cited as a finding
- Severity: medium
- Evidence: MEASURED. bun run bench/mcp-budget.ts --sizes=10,100,1000 on darwin arm64, bun 1.3.13 reproduces the quoted numbers EXACTLY to the decimal: search 3.2/6.4/6.5KB, get_page 29.8KB flat, find_consumers 1.5/15.6/157.1KB, find_canonical 0.2KB flat, lint 1.7/16.0/159.2KB. Extrapolation: 100,000 pages -> lint ~4,077k tokens (20x a 200k window). I dispute none of it. BUT get_page is 29.8KB at every corpus size because the generator gives every page an identical 400-sentence body: I measured that body at 29,889 BYTES, which is the whole 29.8KB. Real pages in examples/climate/knowledge/ run 756 TO 1,783 BYTES - a factor of ~17-40 smaller. get_page is O(1) in corpus size and O(body) in the page, which is correct and unavoidable; the 3.8%-of-context figure is A PROPERTY OF THE SYNTHETIC GENERATOR, NOT OF ACCRETA.
- Where: bench/mcp-budget.ts (the body constant); real pages at examples/climate/knowledge/
- Failure scenario: Not a runtime failure - A REPORTING FAILURE, which for a project whose rule is "no claim without a measurement" is the relevant kind. Someone reads "get_page 29.8KB, 3.8% of a 200k window" and prioritises bounding get_page, THE ONE TOOL ON THIS SURFACE THAT IS ALREADY CORRECTLY BOUNDED (by the page the caller explicitly asked for). Meanwhile check_drift, which the benchmark does not exercise at all, is the one that reaches 533%.
- Falsifiable proposal: State the body size as a declared parameter in the benchmark output, and add check_drift and list_recent_changes to the measured set with a stub adapter - the two tools the file's own header comment names as unbounded but does not measure. Wrong if real KBs carry pages in the tens-of-KB range rather than ~1KB.
- Cost to verify: <1h for the disclosure; ~2h to add the two tools.
- Confidence: high.
- ON WHETHER THE CORPUS IS REPRESENTATIVE OR PESSIMISTIC: it is REPRESENTATIVE IN KIND AND PESSIMISTIC IN DEGREE, and the two need separating. The lint-finding-per-page rate is honest - though note the file's comment claims "at least two lint findings" per page while the measured findings count is exactly N, not 2N, so the comment slightly overstates its own generator. One finding per page is exactly what a half-finished ingest produces, and precisely the state an agent is in when it calls lint, so as a lint input it is FAIR rather than pessimistic. The find_consumers shape is the pessimistic part: every page links to page-000000, making it a PERFECT STAR with N-1 inbound relations. Real link graphs are not stars. The 157.1KB figure is a genuine UPPER BOUND for a maximally-connected hub, not a typical case, and should be reported as such. Both remain the right things to measure, because both are the UNBOUNDED direction, and the point is to find where the cliff is, not where the median sits.

## F-AIE-03: No tool declares outputSchema or returns structuredContent, though the pinned SDK and the negotiated protocol version both support it
- Severity: medium
- Evidence: MEASURED and CITED. Installed SDK is @modelcontextprotocol/sdk@1.30.0 (bun.lock line 94; manifest range ^1.30.0 at packages/mcp-server/package.json:33). In that build, dist/esm/types.js:2 declares LATEST_PROTOCOL_VERSION = '2025-11-25', with SUPPORTED_PROTOCOL_VERSIONS = ['2025-11-25','2025-06-18','2025-03-26','2024-11-05','2024-10-07']. dist/esm/server/mcp.d.ts:154 and :283 accept outputSchema on registration; :257-258 documents the contract as "return structuredContent if the tool has an outputSchema defined, content if it does not"; RegisteredTool carries outputSchema at :270. structuredContent and resource_link are throughout dist/esm/types.d.ts. NOTHING in packages/mcp-server/src/server.ts passes outputSchema - all eight register() calls supply description and inputSchema only, and every one routes through json() at :28-30, emitting a single text block.
  CITED - MCP spec 2025-11-25, server/tools, https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/tools, retrieved 2026-08-10: "If an output schema is provided: Servers MUST provide structured results that conform to this schema. Clients SHOULD validate structured results against this schema." And critically against the backwards-compatibility objection: "For backwards compatibility, a tool that returns structured content SHOULD also return the serialized JSON in a TextContent block." The spec's own worked example shows BOTH fields populated simultaneously.
- Where: packages/mcp-server/src/server.ts:28-30 (json()), and every register call at :40,:59,:74,:97,:107,:119,:133,:146
- Failure scenario: An agent calls check_drift. The three-outcome distinction - which the README and the tool description both go to lengths to teach - ARRIVES AS PROSE INSIDE A TEXT BLOB the model must re-parse from its own context. There is no machine-checkable contract that unresolvable is a FIELD rather than a WORD, so a client cannot programmatically route on it, and a model that mis-reads the blob collapses "I cannot tell" into "nothing changed" - the exact confusion tools.ts:94-96 has a load-bearing comment warning against. The safeguard exists in the data and IS DISCARDED AT THE SERIALISATION BOUNDARY.
- Falsifiable proposal: Add outputSchema to the tools whose shape is stable - check_drift first, since it carries the distinction most worth making machine-checkable - and return BOTH structuredContent and the text block, which the spec explicitly sanctions. Separately, drop the 2-space indent: JSON.stringify(value) instead of JSON.stringify(value,null,2) at server.ts:29. WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: measure the byte delta of removing the indent and the client-side behaviour delta of adding the schema. If compact JSON saves under ~5%, the indent argument is not worth making on token grounds alone; if the clients accreta targets ignore structuredContent entirely, the schema buys only validation and documentation, not agent behaviour. Both directly measurable - the first with ONE LINE changed in the existing benchmark's serialize().
- Cost to verify: ~1h for the indent measurement; ~4h for a schema on one tool plus a client-side check via accreta-atlas/scripts/mcp-smoke.ts, which already drives the server with a real SDK Client.
- Confidence: high on the capability being available and unused - read directly from the installed SDK and current spec. Medium on the size of the benefit, because I did not measure the indent saving and did not test client handling.

## F-AIE-04: Only search_pages is bounded; the other read tools have no limit, no pagination, and no truncation - and MCP defines pagination they could use
- Severity: high
- Evidence: MEASURED and CITED. Bounds established per tool by reading the query layer:
  - search_pages: BOUNDED. search.ts:34 Math.min(Math.max(limit ?? 20,1),50), enforced with LIMIT ? at :61. Measured FLAT at 6.4->6.5KB from 100->1000 pages: the clamp holds.
  - get_page: BOUNDED BY THE PAGE, O(1) in corpus. Single-row WHERE path = ?. Correctly bounded; see F-AIE-02.
  - find_canonical: effectively bounded in OUTPUT (0.2KB flat) BUT BY NOTHING IN THE CODE: findCanonical has no LIMIT, and the alias branch is a full scan with JSON.parse per candidate row. Output stays small only because alias collisions are rare in the synthetic corpus. A term matching many aliases is unbounded.
  - find_consumers: UNBOUNDED. findRelated issues two queries with ORDER BY and no LIMIT, concatenating both directions. Measured 1.5->15.6->157.1KB, linear in relations.
  - check_drift: UNBOUNDED AND QUADRATIC. See F-AIE-01.
  - lint_knowledge_base: UNBOUNDED. lint runs three LIMIT-less queries and pushes up to 4 findings per page. Measured 1.7->16.0->159.2KB. INPUT SCHEMA IS {} (server.ts:138) - THE AGENT CANNOT ASK FOR LESS EVEN IF IT WANTS TO.
  CITED - MCP spec 2025-11-25: tools/list "supports pagination"; resources/list likewise. NOTE PRECISELY WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE ACCRETA: MCP defines cursor pagination for the PROTOCOL'S OWN list operations, not for arbitrary tool results. A paginated lint_knowledge_base must implement its own cursor in the tool's input schema; the protocol supplies the CONVENTION, not the MECHANISM. I want to be exact because it would be easy to overclaim.
- Where: packages/core/src/query/lint.ts:43-113; packages/core/src/query/page.ts:105-123 and :152-193; packages/mcp-server/src/server.ts:138
- Failure scenario: The circular one, WITH THE N COMPUTED. An agent is asked to fix a KB's provenance gaps. It calls lint_knowledge_base - correctly, it is the tool for exactly that. At 1,000 pages with one finding each the response is 159.2KB ~ 40.8k tokens, 20.4% of a 200k window (measured). The agent still has ~80% and can work. AT 5,000 PAGES the response is ~204k estimated tokens: THE ANSWER ALONE NO LONGER FITS IN THE WINDOW, before any pages needing fixing are read, before the system prompt, before the plan. N ~ 4,900 FINDINGS IS WHERE A 200k WINDOW BREAKS (200,000 / ~40.8 tokens per finding, from the measured 159.2KB/1000). The agent that called lint IN ORDER TO FIX the KB is the one that cannot read the answer, and the failure surfaces as the agent appearing to give up - not as an error naming the real cause.
- Falsifiable proposal: Give lint_knowledge_base a limit (default ~50) and an offset or cursor, plus a kind filter over the five existing LintFinding kinds - the report ALREADY carries count and pages_checked separately from findings (tools.ts:105-112), so a truncated response can honestly report the TRUE TOTAL alongside a bounded page. Same for find_consumers. PRESERVE count AS THE UNTRUNCATED NUMBER: reporting a truncated count would be the same class of error as reporting unresolvable as "current". WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: if agents given a paginated lint tool systematically fix FEWER findings per session than agents given the full dump - because paging costs turns and they stop early - then bounding hurts the actual task and the unbounded response was right.
- Cost to verify: ~4h for limit/offset/kind on lint and find_consumers plus tests. The falsification experiment is larger.
- Confidence: high. Bounds read directly from the SQL, sizes measured, and the N is arithmetic over a measured constant.

## F-AIE-05: The stale index is a protocol problem the SDK already solves - listChanged and resource subscriptions exist and are unused
- Severity: medium
- Evidence: MEASURED and CITED. packages/mcp-server/src/context.ts:92 opens the index once, read-only, inside createContext(), which main.ts:8 calls exactly once at startup. There is NO reopen path and NO watcher anywhere in the package. Meanwhile the write tool's own success message says "Written. Run `accreta reindex` for the index to reflect this." (tools.ts:186) - THE SERVER TELLS THE AGENT TO TRIGGER THE VERY OPERATION THAT REPLACES THE FILE UNDERNEATH ITS OWN HANDLE, and then has no way to notice it happened or to say so. The SDK at 1.30.0 exposes sendResourceListChanged(), sendToolListChanged() and sendPromptListChanged() on McpServer (dist/esm/server/mcp.d.ts:202,:206,:210); NONE is called in server.ts.
  CITED - MCP spec 2025-11-25, server/resources, retrieved 2026-08-10: the resources capability supports subscribe ("whether the client can subscribe to be notified of changes to individual resources") and listChanged; servers declaring listChanged SHOULD send notifications/resources/list_changed, and subscribed clients receive notifications/resources/updated with the changed URI. The protocol HAS A FIRST-CLASS ANSWER and it is not being used.
- Where: packages/mcp-server/src/context.ts:92; main.ts:8; tools.ts:186
- Failure scenario: An agent with writes enabled runs the intended loop: check_drift -> finds a stale page -> verifies it -> update_verified_revision -> is told to reindex -> runs accreta reindex. The rebuild writes a new SQLite file and atomically renames it. The server's handle still points at the replaced inode. The agent calls check_drift again TO CONFIRM ITS FIX LANDED, and is told the page is still stale - because the server is reading the pre-rename index. THE AGENT'S CORRECT ACTION REPORTS AS HAVING HAD NO EFFECT. It may re-verify and re-write the same page, or conclude the tool is broken. Nothing in the protocol traffic distinguishes this from genuine drift, and no notification is available to correct it, because the server never declared a capability that would let it send one.
- Falsifiable proposal: (1) Declare resources.listChanged, watch the index path, reopen on change and emit notifications/resources/list_changed. (2) CHEAPER AND INDEPENDENT OF NOTIFICATIONS: stat the index path (or read meta) before serving each request and reopen if the file identity changed - fixes the correctness bug with NO protocol work, and (1) then becomes an optimisation saving a round trip. I think (2) is stronger. WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: if reopening per request costs meaningfully on measured tool latencies, or if the atomic-rename swap already leaves the old handle serving data consistent enough that the scenario cannot occur - verifiable by writing exactly the failing test: build, open read-only, rebuild with a changed page, query through the ORIGINAL handle, assert the change is visible. If it already is, THIS FINDING IS VOID.
- Cost to verify: ~2h for that test. ~1 day for the notification path.
- Confidence: MEDIUM. The code path is certain and the SDK capability is certain, but I DID NOT RUN THE REOPEN TEST - I am read-only and it needs a build-then-rebuild cycle against a live handle. The precise observable behaviour of the stale handle after rename(2) is asserted from the code, not measured, which is why the proposal leads with the test that would settle it.

## F-AIE-06: stdio-only forecloses the hosted deployment the README names, and the SDK ships the two transports that would enable it
- Severity: medium
- Evidence: MEASURED and CITED. packages/mcp-server/src/main.ts:12 connects StdioServerTransport and nothing else; no HTTP transport is imported anywhere. The installed SDK 1.30.0 ships streamableHttp.js, webStandardStreamableHttp.js and sse.js alongside stdio.js in dist/esm/server/ - verified by listing that directory. CITED - MCP spec 2025-11-25, basic/transports, https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/transports, retrieved 2026-08-10: exactly two standard transports are defined, stdio and Streamable HTTP; HTTP+SSE from 2024-11-05 is explicitly the "deprecated HTTP+SSE transport". Crucially the spec ALSO says "Clients SHOULD support stdio whenever possible" - SO STDIO IS NOT A LEGACY CHOICE, IT IS THE RECOMMENDED DEFAULT. accreta's README (line 87) defers "hosted deployment auth and the sync loop" (issue #21) on the stated grounds that it would "encode guesses" about a deployment story that does not exist.
- Where: packages/mcp-server/src/main.ts:12; examples/.mcp.json and packages/mcp-server/README.md, both of which spawn bun run .../main.ts as a subprocess
- Failure scenario: What stdio forecloses, concretely. (a) The KB must live on the same machine as the agent - ACCRETA_ROOT is a local filesystem path and openIndex needs a local SQLite file, so a team sharing one KB cannot point several agents at it without each holding a copy. (b) One client per process: N agents means N processes, N index handles, and N copies of the F-AIE-05 stale-handle problem. (c) The hosted deployment in issue #21 is not reachable by configuration. (d) Every client must have Bun installed and be able to spawn it, coupling the agent-facing surface to ADR-0005's runtime choice.
- Falsifiable proposal: I do NOT propose adding HTTP now. THE README'S REASONING IS SOUND AND I WANT TO SAY SO EXPLICITLY: building a transport for a deployment story that does not exist would encode guesses. The falsifiable claim is narrower - that adding Streamable HTTP later is CHEAP PROVIDED createServer(ctx) stays transport-agnostic, WHICH TODAY IT IS: server.ts:32 returns an McpServer and main.ts alone chooses the transport. That separation is the thing worth protecting, and it is already right. WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: if a Streamable HTTP spike requires changes inside createServer or tools.ts rather than only main.ts - most likely around per-session state, since ToolContext currently holds ONE PROCESS-WIDE db HANDLE that multiple concurrent sessions would share. A real risk and a genuine reason the eventual port may be harder than the clean separation suggests.
- Cost to verify: ~4h for a throwaway spike.
- Confidence: high on what stdio forecloses and on the spec's transport set. Medium on the port being cheap.

## F-AIE-07: Resources would fit a knowledge base, but the client-control argument defeats them for the primary path - and there is a third option neither side covers
- Severity: low
- Evidence: REASONED, with a CITED protocol basis. THIS FINDING REACHES ONLY REASONED FOR ITS CENTRAL JUDGEMENT AND I AM FLAGGING THAT IN ITS OWN SUMMARY. The protocol facts are solid: spec 2025-11-25, server/resources states resources are "APPLICATION-DRIVEN, with host applications determining how to incorporate context", against server/tools stating tools are "MODEL-CONTROLLED, meaning that the language model can discover and invoke tools automatically". That is the whole asymmetry. A KB is definitionally a set of addressable documents, pages.path is already a natural URI key, and getPage is already a URI-shaped lookup. BUT the counter-argument is decisive for the primary path: if pages were resources, whether the agent can read a page depends on whether the HOST chose to surface it, and accreta's core loop must work unattended. WHAT NEITHER SIDE COVERS: the spec permits resource_link content blocks FROM TOOL RESULTS - "A tool MAY return links to Resources... the tool will return a URI that can be subscribed to or fetched by the client" - and resource_link is in the installed SDK's types. So search_pages could return resource links instead of inlined snippets, letting the client fetch bodies only for hits the agent actually wants, WITHOUT any tool becoming client-controlled. The spec notes such links "are not guaranteed to appear in the results of a resources/list request", exactly the property wanted.
- Where: design-level; packages/mcp-server/src/server.ts:40-57; packages/core/src/query/search.ts:55
- Failure scenario: The concrete cost is MODEST, which is why this is low. search_pages at 1,000 pages returns 6.5KB - 20 results each carrying a 16-token snippet. The agent typically wants one or two. It pays for 20 snippets to choose among them, then pays again in a get_page call. With resource links it would pay for 20 URIs plus titles and fetch one body. At current sizes this saves single-digit KB and IS NOT WORTH SCHEDULING. It becomes material only if the result limit rises above 50 or snippets grow.
- Falsifiable proposal: Keep tools as the primary path - resources should NOT replace get_page, and I think the current design is right. Optionally add resource_link blocks alongside the existing text. WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: measure whether the clients accreta targets actually render or act on resource_link blocks. If Claude Code and Cursor ignore them, the links are DEAD WEIGHT and ADD bytes rather than removing them - which would invert the proposal.
- Cost to verify: ~2h to emit the links; the client-handling question I cannot answer from this repository.
- Confidence: LOW-TO-MEDIUM. Protocol facts cited and firm; the judgement about which failure mode matters more rests on an argument about client behaviour I could not measure, and client resource support is precisely what varies most across the ecosystem.

## F-AIE-08: The tool descriptions are strong prompt engineering, with one genuine ambiguity between search_pages and find_canonical
- Severity: low
- Evidence: REASONED from packages/mcp-server/src/server.ts:40-163. REASONED ONLY - assessing descriptions as prompt surface is a judgement, and the honest test is behavioural, not textual. WHAT WORKS: check_drift (:111) enumerates all three outcomes inline with a one-clause gloss each and closes with "Only the absence of all three means 'current'" - it teaches the distinction AT THE POINT OF USE rather than deferring to docs. list_recent_changes (:123) carries the matching warning. find_consumers (:78) names the direction field and defines both values, so the agent knows the discriminator before it sees the payload. search_pages (:44) states the selection rule positively. These are better than typical. THE AMBIGUITY: search_pages is "The primary discovery tool: use it when you do not already know a page's path", and find_canonical is "Resolve a term to the page that authoritatively defines it... Use when you have a name and need the definition rather than a list of mentions." BOTH FIRE ON "I have a name, I do not have a path." The intended split is one-vs-many and defining-vs-mentioning, but an agent holding a term that MAY OR MAY NOT be a page title cannot tell which applies WITHOUT ALREADY KNOWING THE ANSWER. find_canonical returns {count:0,results:[]} on a miss (tools.ts:57-60) with no hint to try search - measured at 0.2KB, so the cheap-probe-first strategy is nearly free, but nothing in the descriptions tells the agent that.
- Where: packages/mcp-server/src/server.ts:44 and :101; miss-path at tools.ts:57-60
- Failure scenario: An agent is asked "what does the KB say about radiative forcing?" It calls search_pages - the description says primary discovery tool - and gets up to 20 hits including the canonical page AND every page mentioning the term. IT SYNTHESISES ACROSS MENTIONS RATHER THAN READING THE PAGE THAT AUTHORITATIVELY DEFINES IT, and produces an answer assembled from passing references while the defining page sits in the result list unread. Nothing errors, the answer is plausible, and it may under-cite the canonical source. For a project whose purpose is provenance back to authoritative sources, silently preferring mentions over the definition is the failure mode that matters, and it is invisible in the output.
- Falsifiable proposal: Add one sentence to find_canonical: that it is cheap, returns empty rather than erring, and should be tried BEFORE search_pages when the input reads like a concept name. Add the converse pointer to its empty result: message: "No page defines this term. Try search_pages for mentions." - the miss path already returns a structured object with room for it. WHAT WOULD PROVE THIS WRONG: run a fixed set of concept-name queries with current and revised descriptions and count how often the agent reaches the canonical page. If the tool-choice distribution does not shift, the descriptions were not the constraint and the ambiguity is theoretical.
- Cost to verify: ~30 min for the text; ~4h for the behavioural A/B.
- Confidence: medium. The textual overlap is plain on reading, but whether models actually mis-route on it is exactly what I could not test, and models are often better at this disambiguation than the text suggests.

## WHAT THIS SURFACE GETS RIGHT
Named explicitly, because a review that only lists faults would misrepresent the design - and each is a place where the easy choice was the wrong one and was not taken.
- THE CONFIRM TOKEN IS A REAL CAPABILITY CHECK, NOT A SPEED BUMP. confirmToken (tools.ts:122-127) hashes path\0revision\0currentValue. INCLUDING THE CURRENT VALUE IS THE LOAD-BEARING PART AND THE PART MOST REVIEWERS WOULD DROP: it means a token cannot be minted without having observed present state, cannot be replayed after the page changes, and cannot be transplanted to a different edit. A confirm:true boolean - the obvious design - lets a model skip straight to writing, and the comment at :114-121 says exactly that. This is the one tool that can rewrite provenance, and it is the one made hardest to call.
- THE WRITE TOOL IS NOT REGISTERED WHEN WRITES ARE DISABLED. server.ts:145 wraps registration in if(ctx.writesEnabled), so a read-only deployment does not advertise a capability it would refuse. Better than returning an error, because a tool that lists but always fails still consumes context in every tools/list and still invites the model to attempt it. accreta-atlas/scripts/mcp-smoke.ts:55-57 asserts the gate FAILS CLOSED, with a comment explaining that a gate failing open would make the audit trail "decorative" - the invariant is tested DOWNSTREAM, FROM OUTSIDE the repository that implements it, which is the right place to test it from.
- THE THREE-WAY DRIFT OUTCOME IS NOT COLLAPSED, AND THE REFUSAL IS ENFORCED IN MORE THAN ONE PLACE. Distinct fields in DriftReport, each with a comment explaining why collapsing would be a claim the system cannot support; restated in the tool description, in the README, and defended again in listRecentChangesTool (tools.ts:94-101) where an exception becomes unresolvable:true rather than an empty list. "I cannot tell" reaching the agent AS ITSELF is the whole discipline of this project, and it survived the trip to the protocol boundary intact. Related: drift.ts skips revisions whose diff is empty, with "Reporting it would train the reader to ignore the report" - a false-positive-suppression judgement a less careful implementation would have gotten backwards.
- THE WRITE TOOL EDITS MARKDOWN AND ASKS FOR A REINDEX RATHER THAN TOUCHING THE INDEX. Upholds "markdown in git is the source of truth" at the one point where violating it would have been convenient. (It is also what creates F-AIE-05 - the fix belongs in the reopen path, not in relaxing this.)
- setFrontmatterField EDITS ONE LINE INSTEAD OF RESERIALISING (tools.ts:190-214), with a comment recording why: a parse-and-reserialize would reorder keys, normalise quotes, and destroy the wikilink syntax the frontmatter preprocessing exists to accommodate.
- packages/core DOES NOT BRANCH ON ADAPTER IDENTITY, AND detectDrift PROVES IT. My F-AIE-01 harness is incidental evidence the abstraction holds: I substituted a stub adapter with no filesystem and no git behind it, and the drift path ran unmodified.

## Experiment card: does bounding lint help or hurt the agent that has to fix the findings
- Question: Does giving lint_knowledge_base a limit and a kind filter increase or decrease the number of findings an agent actually FIXES per session?
- Hypothesis: Bounded wins, and the gap widens with corpus size. At 1,000 pages the unbounded response is 40.8k tokens (20.4%) and the agent still functions; at 5,000 it is ~204k and cannot. Parity at 100 pages, bounded ahead at 1,000, unbounded at zero fixes by 5,000. FAILS IF bounded is WORSE at any size - plausible, because paging costs turns and an agent may stop early believing it is done.
- Method: Use bench/mcp-budget.ts's generator at N in {100,1000,5000}. Variant A current; variant B with limit (default 50), offset and kind, returning the UNTRUNCATED count alongside the bounded page. Same agent, model, window, prompt - "fix every finding the knowledge base reports" - with writes enabled. TEN RUNS PER CELL. Drive through a real SDK Client as accreta-atlas/scripts/mcp-smoke.ts already does.
- Metric: FINDINGS FIXED PER SESSION, verified by re-running lint and differencing count. This and not tokens-consumed, because tokens are the mechanism and fixes are the thing anyone cares about; a variant that used fewer tokens and fixed less would be a regression a token metric would score as a win.
- Falsification criterion: Bounded fixes fewer findings than unbounded at N=1000 (where both fit in context), across ten runs. That would mean pagination cost exceeds context cost in the regime that matters, and F-AIE-04's proposal should be withdrawn in favour of a raised limit or smarter default ordering.
- Cost: ~1 day to build variant B and the harness; 90 agent sessions, ~9M tokens, tens of dollars.
- Not measured: (a) whether the QUALITY of fixes differs - an agent seeing 50 findings may fix them more carefully than one seeing 1,000, and this counts only volume. (b) the check_drift quadratic, which needs its own experiment and a different corpus shape. (c) real-world finding distributions - this corpus gives every page exactly one finding, whereas real KBs CLUSTER, and clustering is what a kind filter exploits, so this design probably UNDERSTATES variant B.

## What I could not establish
- WHETHER THE SHARED-REVISION WORST CASE IN F-AIE-01 IS TYPICAL. My quadratic measurement assumed all pages verified at one revision. A git source produces that naturally - one ingest run, one HEAD - but I could not confirm against a real corpus, because accreta-atlas/kb/knowledge/ is empty by design (verified) and examples/climate/knowledge/ has only 10 pages. Needs a populated multi-page KB over a real git source; then count distinct last_verified_revision values.
- WHETHER THE STALE READ-ONLY HANDLE ACTUALLY SERVES PRE-RENAME DATA. F-AIE-05's failure scenario is REASONED from context.ts:92 plus the atomic-rename swap, NOT OBSERVED. I am read-only and it needs a build-open-rebuild-query cycle. The test is small and written out in the finding; UNTIL IT RUNS, THAT FINDING'S SEVERITY COULD BE ANYTHING FROM MEDIUM TO VOID.
- WHETHER LIU ET AL. TRANSFERS TO TOOL OUTPUT. I checked, and I am DELIBERATELY NOT LEANING ON IT. "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts", Liu, Lin, Hewitt, Paranjape, Bevilacqua, Petroni, Liang - arXiv 2307.03172 (2023), published TACL 2024. Finding: performance is highest when relevant information sits at the beginning or end, and "significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts", in two settings - multi-document QA and key-value retrieval. BOTH SETTINGS ARE RETRIEVED DOCUMENTS PLACED IN A PROMPT, NOT TOOL RESULTS RETURNED MID-CONVERSATION, and the paper studies a NEEDLE AMONG DISTRACTORS whereas a 1,000-finding lint dump is 1,000 NEEDLES the agent must process exhaustively. The mechanism is plausibly the same but the task is not, and I found no study measuring positional degradation over tool output specifically. So I used it ONLY as a directional remark and NOWHERE as load-bearing evidence.
- WHETHER TARGET CLIENTS ACT ON structuredContent OR resource_link. F-AIE-03 and F-AIE-07 both turn on this and neither can be answered from inside these repositories.
- THE ACTUAL TOKEN COUNTS. Everything uses bytes/4, the same method and known bias as bench/mcp-budget.ts - which UNDERSTATES JSON. So every percentage-of-context figure I report, including the 533% for check_drift and the N~4,900 break-even for lint, IS A FLOOR. A real tokenizer would settle it in about an hour and would move every number in the same direction: worse.
