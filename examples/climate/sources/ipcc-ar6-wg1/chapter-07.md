# Chapter 7 — The Earth's Energy Budget, Climate Feedbacks and Climate Sensitivity

## 7.2 Radiative forcing

Effective radiative forcing is the change in net downward radiative flux at the top of the
atmosphere after allowing rapid adjustments, expressed in watts per square metre. A doubling
of carbon dioxide relative to pre-industrial concentrations corresponds to approximately
3.9 W m-2, with an uncertainty of roughly 0.5 W m-2.

Positive forcing indicates energy accumulating in the climate system.

## 7.4 Climate feedbacks

The net feedback parameter is assessed at approximately -1.16 W m-2 per degree Celsius. The
water vapour and lapse rate feedbacks together are strongly positive; the cloud feedback
remains the largest single contributor to uncertainty.

## 7.5 Equilibrium climate sensitivity

Equilibrium climate sensitivity is the eventual change in global mean surface temperature
following a sustained doubling of carbon dioxide. This assessment gives a best estimate of
3 degrees Celsius, a likely range of 2.5 to 4, and a very likely range of 2 to 5.

The narrowing relative to earlier assessments comes from combining process understanding,
the instrumental record, and paleoclimate evidence, rather than from any single line.
